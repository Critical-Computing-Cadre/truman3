# Kinds of site

Every study is one of six kinds of site. The kind decides how the pages are laid out and which features start switched on. You choose it on the **New study** page, under **What kind of platform is it?**

::: warning The kind is permanent
The kind can't change once the study starts. The features can, in **Settings**.
:::

## The six kinds

### Classic

![A classic feed on a wide screen, with the participant's profile beside the posts](/shots/kind-classic-wide.png)

Classic looks like a community feed, such as Facebook or LinkedIn. Posts sit on cards in one column, with the person's name and picture at the top, a count of likes, and a row of buttons along the bottom. Comments are open under each post, so people read and reply without leaving the feed.

It starts with writing posts, comments, likes, flagging, following, blocking, reporting, and notifications. Everything else is off until you turn it on.

### Photo

![A photo app on a wide screen, with a menu down the left and stories across the top](/shots/kind-photo-wide.png)

Photo looks like a photo app in a browser, such as Instagram. Square photos fill the column, with icons under each, who liked it, and the caption. Stories run across the top and open full screen. A profile is a grid of photos, and Explore is a grid of photos from people the participant doesn't follow.

On top of what Classic has, it starts with stories, highlights, close friends, places, tagging people, the profile and Explore grids, follower counts, reposts, hashtags, mentions, who to follow, and a page for each post, among others.

### Short posts

![A short-posts app on a wide screen, with trending topics and who to follow](/shots/kind-short-posts-wide.png)

Short posts looks like a microblog, such as Bluesky or X. There's a menu of icons down the left, a narrow column of short posts, and small icons with counts under each. People pass posts along or quote them, and replies are on each post's own page.

On top of what Classic has, it starts with a following feed, reposts, quote posts, hashtags, mentions, trending, who to follow, view counts, badges, polls, link posts, context notes, and labels on posts, among others.

### Forum

![A forum on a wide screen, with a list of communities beside the posts](/shots/kind-forum-wide.png)

Forum looks like a discussion site, such as Reddit. Each post names its community, has a title, and has votes beside it. People open a post to read its comments, which nest as threads. Communities have their own pages, with a banner, rules, and moderators.

It has up and down votes where the others have likes, and following is off. It starts with spaces (the communities), titles, karma, flair, crossposting, polls, link posts, pinned and locked posts, and a public mod log, among others.

### Short video

![A short-video site on a wide screen, with one tall video in the middle and a menu down the left](/shots/kind-short-video-wide.png)

Short video looks like a short-video app, such as TikTok or Reels. One video fills the screen and plays by itself, and swiping up goes to the next. On a computer, the arrow keys or the scroll wheel do the same. The author, likes, comments, save, and share run down the right side, and what the post says is along the bottom. Comments slide up over the video, which keeps playing. A tap on the video pauses it.

Videos start without sound, because browsers don't allow sound until someone taps the page. One tap on **Tap for sound** turns it on for every video after. In **Settings**, **How do videos get their sound?** can instead ask for a tap before the first video, so sound is on from the start.

Posts here should be videos. See [Pictures and videos](/guide/posts-and-cast#pictures-and-videos) for how to add them. A post with a photo shows the photo, and one with only words shows them on a colored card, so nothing goes missing.

It starts with likes, comments, saving, sharing, following, the profile and Explore grids, hashtags, mentions, and messages off until you turn them on. Moderation is off too.

The sample content is nine short science clips cut from NASA's public-domain footage: a solar flare, Perseverance landing on Mars, the Artemis I launch, Earth from the space station, and more. Each post credits NASA. Their sound is replaced with soft music, so **Tap for sound** has something to play.

### Video platform

![A video site on a wide screen, with a grid of thumbnails and a menu down the left](/shots/kind-video-wide.png)

Video platform looks like a video site, such as YouTube. Home is a grid of thumbnails, each with the video's length, its title, the channel, and its views. Pressing one opens its page: the video starts, with the title, the channel with a **Subscribe** button, likes, the description, and comments under it.

**Up next** runs down the side. It's the rest of the feed, in the order you set for the feed, so what a participant is "recommended" is in your hands, and can differ by condition. When a video ends, the next one starts after a five-second countdown, which they can cancel, or they can turn **Autoplay** off. Your data records how each video was opened: from the grid (`feed`), from Up next (`upNext`), or by itself (`autoplay`), in `content.opened`. Subscribing is following, so it's `actor.followed`.

Posts here should be videos with titles. See [Pictures and videos](/guide/posts-and-cast#pictures-and-videos) for adding them.

The sample content is seven NASA science videos with made-up channels, credited in each description. Their sound is replaced with soft music. Pre-roll ads are coming.

Photo, Short posts, and Forum also start with the moderation features on: removing, warning, banning, answering flags and reports, and automatic rules. They do nothing until you write some moderation. See [Moderation](/guide/moderation).

## Choosing

On the **New study** page, each card shows the same sample post drawn the way that kind draws it, which makes the six easy to compare. Press **Read more** on a card for a description and the full list of **What people can do**. If you still can't decide, **Help me decide** asks five questions about how the platform works and suggests a card:

- Do people belong to communities, each with its own page?
- Is every post a short video, watched one at a time?
- Are posts videos, each opened on its own page with more to watch beside it?
- Is every post a photo?
- Are posts short, and passed along with reposts?

Answer no to all five and it suggests Classic.

Choosing **Sample content** on the same page copies in made-up people and posts that use what that kind has, so you can see it working before you write anything. Each sample also has a few [beats](/guide/beats): someone sends a hello as you arrive and a couple of people follow you, so Messages and Notifications have something in them. They're on the Beats page, to change or delete like the rest.

## Turning features on and off

The kind only sets where you start. To change what the site has, press **Settings** in the builder. The **Study and platform** card has a list under **What's on this platform?** with a tick box for each feature. Untick **Comments** and nobody can comment. Tick **Direct messages** and participants get a Messages page.

The same card has:

- **Name of the site**, which is what participants see as the name of the platform
- **What kind of platform is this?** with **Mixed** (posts can have text, a photo, or both), **Photo** (every post needs a photo), or **Text** (posts can't have photos)
- **Light or dark?** Classic, Forum, and Video platform start light, and Photo, Short posts, and Short video start dark. Either way it's the same for every participant, unless you choose **Whatever their device uses**.
- **Main color**, for the site's name, links, and buttons. Each kind comes with its own: blue for Classic, pink for Photo, teal for Short posts, orange for Forum, violet for Short video, and green for Video platform. Pick another to match your logo, or to make two of your studies look like different sites.
- **How do comment boxes appear?** with **Always open** or **After pressing Reply**
- **The most a participant can write in a post or comment**. A short posts site might use 280.

Press **Save changes** on the card when you're done.

### Menus and small print for show

A real site has more on the page than posts: a search box, a long menu, a place for messages in the corner, and small print such as About and Help. Each kind of site draws these the way that kind of platform does, so the study looks like a site people have been using.

Most of it is only for show. Pressing one shows a short note that it isn't available right now, as a real site does when part of it is down. The press is recorded as a `decor.pressed` event, with what was pressed and the page they were on. See [Your data](/guide/data).

Some become real when your study has the page for them. Then they open it, and aren't for show any more.

**Choosing what's shown.** Under the list of features, the **Study and platform** card has **What's shown for show?** It lists everything your kind of site draws, grouped by where on the page it is.

- Untick anything you don't want.
- Under **Your own**, type a name, such as "Coupons", and press **Add**. It goes in the site's menu with its first letter as its icon. You can add up to 8.

To have none of it, untick **Menus and small print for show** under **What's on this platform?**

## A few features worth knowing about

- **Direct messages** are off in every kind until you turn them on. They're between the participant and one person in the cast.
- **Spaces** are communities that posts belong to. Once they're on, a **Spaces** button appears in the builder. **Stories** get a **Stories** button the same way.
- **Ads** are posts you mark with a label such as Sponsored. Clicking their link is recorded.
- **Adult content tags** cover a post until someone clicks it. There's no age check, so talk to your IRB first.

## The feed

The **Feed** card in Settings controls how the feed behaves for everyone: **How long is the study? (days)**, **What order is the feed in?**, how far back the feed reaches, and the like counts posts start with.

None of these have hidden defaults copied from a real platform. If your study doesn't turn a behavior on, it doesn't happen. Whatever you chose is saved with the version, so you can report it in your methods.
